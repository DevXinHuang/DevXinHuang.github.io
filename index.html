Roman CGI-Adjacent Research

A central thread of my work is research adjacent to the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Coronagraph Instrument (Roman CGI).

Rather than treating detectability as a binary label, I am interested in the structure underneath it:

  • Which planets are actually detectable with Roman CGI?
  • How do phase angle, wavelength, cloud physics, and atmospheric structure shape observability?
  • Where do common detectability assumptions break down?

My work involves constructing and interrogating model grids that connect:

  • Atmospheric structure and composition
  • Reflected versus thermal flux
  • Viewing geometry and phase angle
  • Instrumental sensitivity limits

The goal is not just to produce plots, but to understand the assumptions embedded in them, and to refine detectability arguments so they are transparent, reproducible, and physically grounded.

Roman CGI sits at a particularly interesting boundary: between theory and instrumentation, ambition and feasibility. That boundary is where I enjoy working most.

Computation as a Research Tool

Computation is central to how I think about science.

I use Python-based scientific workflows to:

  • Explore large parameter spaces
  • Build reproducible model grids
  • Use visualization as a diagnostic tool, not just a presentation layer

I am especially interested in computational work that sharpens physical intuition rather than replacing it, where every result can be traced back to a meaningful assumption.

Amateur Radio, Engineering & Field Work

Outside the classroom and lab, I am deeply involved in amateur radio and student-led engineering projects.

I am a General-class amateur radio operator (Call Sign: N7XIN) and serve as Vice President of the University of Arizona Amateur Radio Club (K7UAZ).

Through the club, I help organize:

  • Weekly radio nets and technical outreach
  • High-altitude PicoBalloon experiments
  • Field deployments that combine RF engineering, atmospheric conditions, and real-world constraints

Amateur radio has strongly influenced how I approach research: signals are fragile, environments are messy, and theory only matters if it survives reality.
